What a taper is

A taper is a planned drop in training volume before a race while you keep the intensity. Less total work, same quality. Fatigue goes down faster than fitness, so you arrive at the start line rested and still sharp.

HYROX guide

What to cut and what to keep

  • Cut volume: roughly 30–50% less total running distance and fewer station sets than in your peak weeks.
  • Keep intensity: short efforts at race pace and slightly faster, so your body remembers the speed.
  • Keep frequency: train on similar days as before. Fewer sessions often make people feel sluggish.
  • Drop heavy new stimuli: no max-effort lifts, no new exercises, no full race simulations.

HYROX guide

Race week checklist

  • Sleep more than usual, especially two nights before the race.
  • Eat normally with a bit more carbohydrate in the last two days. Nothing new.
  • Check your start time, division and the latest rulebook.
  • Pack shoes and kit you have trained in.
  • Plan your pacing: first two runs slower than you want.

FAQ

Common questions.

How long should a HYROX taper be?

Most athletes do well with 7–14 days. Beginners racing their first HYROX can use about one week, athletes after a long, heavy block often benefit from two.

Should I stop training before HYROX?

No. Keep training, but do less. Stopping completely often makes you feel flat on race day. Reduce volume and keep short, sharp efforts.

Should I do a full HYROX simulation in race week?

No. A full simulation takes days to recover from. Keep it to short parts of the race at race pace.

What should I eat before a HYROX race?

A light, carb-based meal 2–3 hours before your start that you have tested in training. Avoid new foods on race day.
